# Ethics and sustainability

We try to build Deaftly ethically and sustainably. That's hard to do completely in tech, so this page sets out what we do, what we don't, and the trade-offs we've made, so you can choose with your eyes open.

## Made with deaf people

Deaftly is designed and tested with deaf people, starting with a deaf collaborator who uses it every day. We build what helps in real moments, at a counter or in a waiting room, not what looks good in a demo.

## Accessibility

This site and the app are built to WCAG 2.2 AA. We test every page automatically for accessibility problems, in light and dark themes and at phone sizes, and we design for large text, high contrast and clear, plain English.

## No tracking

There's no tracking, advertising or analytics on this site or in the app, and no cookies on this site. We don't sell or share data, and we don't build profiles.

## Who we buy from

We support the [Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions (BDS) movement](https://bdsmovement.net). Before we adopt any package, tool, framework or service, we screen it against BDS's consumer boycott targets and its No Tech for Oppression, Apartheid or Genocide campaign.

Where a tool is the only practical choice and appears on those lists, we only use it if we can do so without paying, so no money goes to an organisation on the list, and we keep looking for an alternative. Everything else we use has passed the screening.

## Sustainability

- **A light site.** This site is plain pages built ahead of time, with no frameworks running in your browser. The fonts are trimmed to the characters we use and served from this site.
- **No servers sitting idle.** The site only uses computing power when someone visits.
- **An efficient app.** Deaftly does its work on your phone, and reading text with the camera happens on the phone too.

## Building with AI

We build Deaftly with the help of AI tools. It wouldn't exist otherwise. AI uses real energy and water, both to train the models and to run them. We've accepted that impact so we could build something useful for deaf people. The footprint of a small app like this is modest, but it isn't zero, and we'd rather say so.

## Open about how it's made

Deaftly is built in public, and every release is signed with the same key, so you can check that the app you install is the real one. See [how to check it](/download/#how).

# At the doctor's

Communicating at the doctor's when you're deaf often goes wrong before the appointment starts: at the reception desk, when your name is called, or when someone talks to you from behind a screen. Deaftly helps with the parts you can control.

## Checking in

Use the **At the doctor's** template. Fill in your appointment time and how you'll communicate, then show it at reception:

> Hi! I'm deaf. I have an appointment at **10:30**. I will be communicating by **typing on my phone**. Please **type on my phone**.

If you've booked a BSL interpreter, the **BSL interpreter** template asks whether they've arrived yet.

## Asking how they'll call you

Ask reception to come and get you instead of calling your name. Type it with **Type & show** and hold it up, so it's in large, clear text they can read from across the desk.

## During the appointment

**Side by side** lets you and the doctor take turns typing on one phone. Each of you gets your own colour, laid out like a script, so it's easy to look back at what was said. Clear prompts show the doctor where to type.

If you'd rather keep hold of your phone, **Face to face** turns your words towards them as you type.

## At the pharmacy

The **Pharmacy** template says you're collecting a prescription and who it's for. To get medicine names right, make your own template with a gap filled by the **camera**, then read the name off the label or letter.

## Tips

- Ask your surgery to record that you're deaf and how you'd like them to contact you, so it's flagged when you book.
- Ask for a longer appointment if you need more time.
- Make your own template for things you say at every visit, such as your date of birth or your usual pharmacy.
